# Active sampling and oversampling evidence for v0.4

Read-only Trackio inspection on September 30, 2026, using
`trackio.Api(server_url="https://trackio.carbonteq.com")`, project
`posttrain-lab`, run configs and unsampled scalar history. These are observed
recipe comparisons, not controlled estimates of an individual feature's effect.

The first recipe pair records about 63% fewer sequential rounds, 44% less
rollout time, and 40% shorter whole updates (1.68x observed update throughput).
Both select the same model, environment, training binding and rollout binding,
with eight groups of four attempts. Their framework/project source digests
differ. The old resolved-settings projection omits the adaptive controller,
although the settings selection names distinguish random and adaptive recipes.
This supports a historical recipe comparison, not an isolated causal claim.

The VORTEX v3/v4 pair has fewer rounds but slightly longer whole updates in v4.
The reply cap also increases from 4096 to 8192 tokens and the prompt cap from
20480 to 24576. The recorded v4 settings projection carries older curriculum
fields despite the yield-first selection name. Do not call this a policy-only
benchmark or infer a speedup from refill counts alone.

The SAMPO pair retains a configured optimizer population of 24 groups of six
attempts. The later run explicitly records `oversample: 4` and
`oversample_refill: 5`; it generates 169.86 candidate episodes per update on
average, versus 156 in the earlier run. Thus fewer rounds do not mean fewer
episodes. Mean rollout time is about 21% lower, but the later run also changes
to a base-model start, FP16, temperature 0.8, learning rate 6e-5, 180 concurrent
slots and a larger KV cache. The earlier run continues an adapter, uses
temperature 0.5, learning rate 5e-5 and 144 slots. Model quality and convergence
are separate questions.

## Refill work and throughput adjustment

Each pair uses the same configured useful optimizer population: 32 episodes
for random/adaptive, 40 for VORTEX v3/v4, and 144 for SAMPO. Compare within a
pair; these are different training recipes. A refill round is any collection
round after the initial round. Extra candidate episodes equal all attempted
episodes minus the configured useful population; they include discarded or
unusable work and are not necessarily generated during a refill.

| Recipe | Updates | Mean refill rounds | Candidate episodes / update | Extra candidate episodes / update | Refill-only seconds / update |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | 20 | 4.55 | 76.40 | 44.40 | 840.11 |
| Adaptive | 20 | 1.05 | 39.80 | 7.80 | 311.80 |
| VORTEX v3, 4K reply cap | 20 | 2.60 | 64.20 | 24.20 | 394.90 |
| VORTEX v4, 8K reply cap | 20 | 1.55 | 53.80 | 13.80 | 293.57 |
| SAMPO continuation | 39 | 0.923 | 156.00 | 12.00 | Not separately recorded |
| SAMPO FP16/base, oversample 4/refill 5 | 100 | 0.050 | 169.86 | 25.86 | Not separately recorded |

The historical GRPO logs preserve per-round timing, so refill-only seconds sum
all rounds after the first. The SAMPO update totals do not preserve per-round
token counts; do not estimate refill time by multiplying average update time
by the fraction of rounds that were refills.

For a throughput adjustment, reconstruct the logged completion-token work
as `tokens = rollout_seconds × rollout_tokens_per_second` for each matching
timing record, then sum over the update. This matches the observer's rate
definition. Completion IDs include tool observations as well as sampled model
tokens. Effective throughput includes environment/tool latency and concurrency;
it is not a pure GPU decoding-speed measurement. Use a time-weighted rate,
`sum(tokens) / sum(seconds)`, rather than averaging round rates.

As an independent consistency check, every paired timing record also agrees
with `rollout_selected_tokens / rollout_selected_token_fraction` to within
1e-5 tokens: 111, 41, 72, 51, 39 and 100 records respectively. This checks the
recorded rate's token denominator without substituting sampled-policy tokens.

| Recipe | Mean completion-token work / update | Effective tokens/s | Observed rollout seconds / update | Seconds at the first recipe's effective rate in its pair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Random | 489,524 | 436.66 | 1121.06 | 1121.06 |
| Adaptive | 248,752 | 395.61 | 628.79 | 569.67 |
| VORTEX v3, 4K reply cap | 659,243 | 1072.04 | 614.94 | 614.94 |
| VORTEX v4, 8K reply cap | 691,800 | 1230.44 | 562.24 | 645.31 |
| SAMPO continuation | 1,171,891 | 4152.12 | 282.24 | 282.24 |
| SAMPO FP16/base, oversample 4/refill 5 | 1,357,189 | 6108.50 | 222.18 | 326.87 |

The last column is a bookkeeping scenario: divide mean completion-token work
by the first run's effective rate within each pair. It holds only the rate
constant; it does not simulate the same concurrency, trajectory lengths,
tool latency, curriculum or model quality. At this common rate, adaptive uses
49% less token work, VORTEX v4 uses 5% more, and recent SAMPO uses 16% more.
Thus VORTEX v4 and oversampled SAMPO reduce sequential rounds without reducing
total token work. Their observed rollout-time gains also involve higher
effective throughput. Adaptive's measured refill-only cost falls 63%, from
840 to 312 seconds, and refill completion-token work falls 71%, from 308,529
to 89,651 tokens per update.

## Aggregation and reproduction

For each run, call `summary()` to locate metric names, then
`history(keys=[...], scalar_only=True, drop_empty=True)`. Preserve `step` and
`timestamp`. Use these metric keys:

- `train/rl/active_sampling_generation_rounds`
- `train/rl/active_sampling_generated_rows`
- `train/rl/active_sampling_oversampled_groups`
- `train/rl/active_sampling_discarded_groups`
- `train/rl/time/rollout_seconds`
- `train/step_time_seconds`

Group by logical `step`. Rounds and whole-update seconds each occur once per
reported update in this snapshot. Older GRPO runs publish rollout seconds once
per candidate-generation round: sum every distinct recorded occurrence within
the update before averaging across updates. Taking only the last round would
understate the cost. The newer SAMPO records already publish one aggregate
rollout-time value per update. No resampling or interpolation is used; include
the first update and report actual coverage rather than requested budgets.

Legacy `candidate_groups_*` counters count episode rows, despite their names.
The later `candidate_groups_retained` counter includes useful oversupply before
trimming; do not treat it as the final optimizer population.

The active September 30 SAMPO run had only 13 reported updates at inspection
time (12 one-round updates). It is excluded from the headline comparison
because the snapshot is partial and will change.
