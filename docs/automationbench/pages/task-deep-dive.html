How a 4B model went from 28% to 51% on unseen tasks

We trained a small model to do office-automation work. This page shows what moved, task by task, and which training data moved it.

6 · Why SFT helped

It learned the workflows in its 97 examples, and reused them on similar test tasks

Test tasks that resemble a training example gained about twice as much as tasks that resemble none. Tasks unlike every example gained least.

      10 · What SDPO did

      SDPO learned from its own mistakes, and one round was enough

      One round on its own attempts, each failed attempt read with written feedback, took the SFT model from 42.4% to 50.6% on test120. More rounds, and the other SDPO runs, added less.

      A training attempt, turn by turn

      The teacher is the model itself, re-reading the attempt with the outcome and the feedback. The median failed attempt is 8 turns long and its first located mistake is at turn 5, so about 70% of its turns are trained.

      Most first mistakes were a missing or wrong action

      2 failed attempts had no feedback from the engine and were left out.

      What we take away

      1. Feedback at the first wrong decision works. Training a failed attempt only up to its first located mistake gave the largest gain of any SDPO run.
      2. One round was best. Each later round of the same run scored lower on held-out tasks.
      3. The gain came with a cost on the warning set. guard10 fell, so the gain is not free (see below).
      4. One run, one seed. 50.6% against the 397B’s 49.6% is not a measured difference.

      Decision, 24 Nov: SDPO stays at 50.6%, the round-1 model of 22 Nov.

      More detail: how it was trained, guard10, and what published work says
      • Start: the SFT model. Tasks: 78 training tasks (48 it sometimes solved, 30 it never solved). Attempts: 8 per task, 624 in all; 622 trained.
      • Teacher: a running average of the student (1% per update), shown the outcome and, for failures, the engine’s feedback with values the attempt never saw hidden.
      • Loss: reverse KL on the teacher’s top 20 tokens plus the tail, importance weight capped at 2; trained on reasoning, tool calls and the act-or-reply token. 39 updates of 16 attempts, learning rate 3e-5, LoRA rank 16.
      • guard10: the 10-task warning set fell from 78.8% (SFT) to 66.3% (SDPO round 1).
      • Published work: SDPO was published for single-turn tasks (Hübotter et al., 2026); its multi-turn, tool-using use is open. Here the feedback had to be located at a turn, and hidden values had to stay hidden.
      Success
      Every required check of the task passes (AutomationBench’s task_completed_correctly).
      Gained / lost
      The solve count moved by 2 or more of 8. Smaller moves are within run-to-run noise.
      Solved (chapter 4)
      At least 4 of 8 attempts; for the 397B at least 1 of 2.
      Causes
      Groupings are measured; “why” statements are our reading of them (Inference), not experiments.
      Grouped fairly
      A task’s level (never, sometimes or always solved) comes from half of the SFT model’s 8 tries; the change is measured on the other half, both ways round. Using the same tries for both would fake gains on never-solved tasks.
