Learning assumptions and what the controller can see

This is a synthetic learner, not an LLM or a GRPO optimizer. Each task has a hidden probability of success. Four independent answers are sampled at fixed probabilities during every initial/refill round. Only a model-update event changes those probabilities.

Steady learning starts tasks at 10%–88% solve probability. Arithmetic starts at 60%–88%, algebra at 35%–63%, geometry at 10%–38%, and probability at 45%–73%. These are assumed abilities of this synthetic learner, not properties of the task categories. Different starting abilities uses an alternative population from 15% to 88%, with the same learning rate. Different learning speeds keeps that alternative population but assigns rates of 0.07, 0.14, or 0.28. The ordinary default rate is 0.14; all rates are illustrative.

A practiced mixed group moves its task toward 99.5% success. Learning follows signal additionally scales the gain by 4p̄(1 − p̄): two successes out of four give signal 1; one or three give 0.75; constant outcomes give 0. A task with current probability p receives direct gain 0.14 × (0.995 − p) × signal in that preset. The other ordinary presets give the same direct gain for any mixed group. Small transfer gains affect other tasks in the same class and are also signal-scaled in that preset.

These are assumed learning laws, not the GRPO gradient. The transfer stress case also lets algebra practice improve geometry. The forgetting case lowers arithmetic success after update 10. In the noise case, probability tasks remain at 50% regardless of practice.

The controller predicts mixed-group probability using a 41-point probability grid. Class priors average one first-discovery group per distinct task from the last eight steps and mix in 20% uniform prior mass. Each task updates a prior that excludes its own evidence with up to four recent groups. These are illustrative tracking approximations, not calibrated statistical estimates. The 20% prior smoothing is fixed in this toy model and is separate from both exploration controls.

Ten groups are the target batch. OLMo-style mode retains only mixed groups and permits up to four candidate rounds; failure to fill the batch stops the simulation without a model update. Every candidate has a unique task ID within its step. Discovery counts carry across requests; exhausted unseen inventory is reported and reserved slots become reassessment.

Tile color shows average normalized within-group variance, 4p̄(1 − p̄), from orange (0) through neutral (0.5) to green (1). The purple bar shows recent mean reward, equal to success rate for these binary outcomes. Alternating all-success and all-failure groups can therefore give an orange tile with a half-full bar. Grey tiles have no recent evidence; a dash is not a zero. Bars animate between observed values without smoothing or forcing an upward trend. These observations do not establish mastery. Class forecasts may revert toward the weak prior as observations expire. High variance can reflect noise rather than learning.

Lane percentages are realized shares of the current candidate request, before any OLMo-style filtering. Discovery includes reserved and additional unseen tasks. Recheck comes from familiar-task reassessment, including class coverage and exhausted discovery slots; it has no separate quota. Selection is displayed as one whole request, then generation, observation, and update. The engine still draws tasks conditionally without replacement. No preset demonstrates that this sampler outperforms another.

The controller never receives these hidden values. Settings restart the replay at the selected seed. Reference-probe costs, token costs, optimizer noise, correlated answers, and real model generalization are not modeled.
