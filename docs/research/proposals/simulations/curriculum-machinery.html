What is real, and what is illustrative?

All probabilities are calculated from the displayed toy evidence. The eight draws per batch are scripted, so counts need not match expected proportions. The first batch covers every task for illustration; the actual policy does not require this warm-up.

Task scores average normalized within-task variance from their last two shown visits. Class scores average their two task scores with fixed equal weights. At both levels, 20% equal exploration is mixed with 80% score-proportional sampling; all-zero scores fall back to equal shares. Missing evidence starts without an adaptive bonus.

Real use requires sufficient fresh representative evidence and uncertainty handling. The shown evidence is assumed adequate solely for this illustration. The progress-driven policy is a separate proposal; variance is useful relative signal, not guaranteed learning value.

Inventory colors describe observed state, not class identity. Small bars show success history. Consistent observed success does not prove learning saturation; this toy evidence does not support a mastery claim. Hover over a task for counts and the last observed step. Highlighted outlines indicate selection independently of state color.

Green answers pass; red answers fail. Constant-reward groups have zero outcome-relative advantages, but other groups and KL can still affect the model. No group is discarded and refilled. The step-20 revisit is a scripted exploration draw, not a promised deadline. Later answers come from the later student, not replayed outcomes.
